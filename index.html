Section 01

What ORS Is Actually Hiring For

The posting says Sales Manager. The role requires something more.

Based on the job description and the commercial situation at ORS, the business needs a commercial leader. Someone who takes the sales function completely off the General Manager's desk, runs it with structure and discipline, and hands him a clean picture every week without being asked.

Lead Outside Sales
Lead 6 outside reps (Quebec to BC) and 1 National Account Manager. This is a coaching job before it is a sales job. The first 30 days are diagnostic — where is each rep, what is their conversion rate, what does their pipeline look like, and what do they need to improve.
Launch Inside Sales
Build from zero. Hire, script, structure, measure. The inside team is the commercial engine that multiplies the outside team's effectiveness. CRM discipline starts here.
Own Supplier Relationships
650 brands. The General Manager cannot manage all of them. I set the annual growth targets with priority suppliers, run the business reviews, track joint customer activity, and make sure ORS shows up as a structured commercial partner.
Drive Customer Profitability
Revenue without margin is noise. I work with the General Manager to build the profitability reporting, identify accounts requiring improvement plans, and redirect commercial resources toward the highest-upside distributors.
Build the Commercial System
CRM adoption, pipeline discipline, weekly reporting, forecasting accuracy. The General Manager should be able to walk into any board meeting and describe the commercial position without preparing. That is my job.
Cross-functional Leadership
CS, marketing, purchasing, the US-based Director of Buying Groups. I am the commercial connector — removing roadblocks, aligning priorities, and making sure the team delivers a consistent customer experience.

Section 03

How I Structure Commercial Ownership

What a well-run commercial function takes off leadership's desk.

In every engagement I have run, the measure of success is the same: leadership stops managing the commercial function and starts receiving it. This is the operating model I have built before and would bring to any role.

In a well-run commercial function, leadership stops…
−Following up with Territory Sales Reps on activity levels or pipeline updates
−Being the escalation point for distributor issues or pricing exceptions
−Preparing for supplier business reviews without a commercial summary ready
−Asking what the current pipeline is or when a deal is expected to close
−Managing inside sales activity because the function does not yet exist
−Producing board-level commercial summaries from memory or spreadsheets
And starts…
✓Receiving the Monday morning commercial report — pipeline, activity, priorities — before asking
✓Being consulted on strategy. Commercial execution runs without the GM present
✓Seeing supplier performance data before supplier meetings. Reviews are already structured
✓Receiving weekly pipeline by rep with variance to plan explained and actioned
✓AI-assisted reporting and dashboards replacing manual data gathering — saving hours weekly
✓Receiving an executive-ready commercial summary suitable for senior leadership review
Section 04

How I Assess — The First 30 Days

I do not arrive with answers. I arrive with the right questions.

Before changing anything I need to understand what is actually happening — not what reports say, not what people think. These are the diagnostic questions I bring in week one.

The Team
Where does each rep spend their time? How much is in front of customers vs. administration?
What does CRM health look like — completeness, staging accuracy, activity logging?
What is each rep's conversion rate from meeting to proposal to close?
What does coaching look like today? How often, what format, what gets measured?
Where do reps feel they need more support — product, pricing, process, or tools?
The Pipeline
How reliable is the current forecast? What is the variance between forecast and close over the last 6 months?
Which accounts have the most pipeline potential that is not currently being pursued?
Where is pipeline stalling? Which stage loses the most deals and why?
Customer Profitability
Which accounts drive the most margin? Which consume the most service time for the least return?
What does wallet share look like per distributor — what are we getting versus what they could give?
Which accounts are declining and why? Is this addressable or structural?
Are there customer segments or verticals that are under-served by the current coverage model?
Suppliers
Which 10 suppliers represent the greatest margin opportunity for ORS Canada?
What does a supplier business review look like today — cadence, format, who attends, what gets decided?
Are there suppliers investing in joint customer programs that we are not fully capturing?
Which supplier relationships need to be rebuilt or restructured?

Section 09

The CRM Architecture I Build

What goes in. What comes out. What leadership sees.

CRM fails when it is seen as an administrative burden. It produces when it is built around what the rep needs to do their job and what leadership needs to make decisions. This is the structure I have implemented and refined across multiple commercial builds.

Account Records
Account tier (A/B/C) by revenue and margin
Wallet share estimate vs. actual
Last visit date and next scheduled visit
Key contacts and relationship owner
Product mix — what they buy vs. what they could buy
Any open service issues or pricing exceptions
Annual revenue target and YTD performance
Pipeline Management
5 deal stages: Prospect → Qualified → Proposed → Negotiating → Closed
Required fields at each stage before advancing
Close probability and weighted value by stage
Expected close date — mandatory field, reviewed weekly
Activity log — every call, email, visit recorded same day
Competitive notes — why we win or lose
Inside team pipeline tracked in a separate view
What Leadership Sees
Monday pipeline dashboard — total, by rep, by stage, vs. last week
Rep activity scorecard — calls, visits, proposals vs. targets
Forecast view — weighted pipeline vs. monthly revenue plan
Customer profitability report — top 20 accounts by margin contribution
Account health alerts — any A-tier account with no visit in 45 days
Inside team KPI board — live activity and pipeline
Supplier pipeline — deals tied to priority supplier products

Section 11

How I Manage Supplier Relationships at ORS

650 brands. Most get insufficient commercial attention.

No one person can manage 650 supplier relationships. The work is to identify which 10–15 suppliers represent the greatest commercial opportunity for ORS Canada and build structured, joint business plans with each of them.

Tier 1
Strategic Partners — 5–8 suppliers
Annual joint business plan — mutual growth targets, named customer targets, marketing investment
Co-branded promotions, product launches, and field sales support scheduled annually
Quarterly business reviews — GM and I attend, supplier VP or Canada lead present
Exclusive or preferential pricing agreements tied to volume performance
Monthly pipeline tracking — deals tied to their product line tracked in CRM
Tier 2
Growth Partners — 15–20 suppliers
Semi-annual business reviews — I lead, supplier regional manager attends
Product training for inside and outside reps scheduled twice per year
Annual growth targets set and tracked quarterly
Inside team calling campaigns aligned with supplier promotional calendar
Tier 3
Transactional — Remainder
Order fulfillment and pricing managed by purchasing
Annual review of performance and margin contribution
Any supplier whose margin or volume drops below threshold triggers a review
Potential candidates for rationalization if they create operational complexity without commercial return
